RRR Prediction Audit

This audit asks whether the RRR method, its matched-fire lists, or any rule learned from the history of operations can predict the next result. It uses the live records, all 29 charts, and a check that the scanner itself works.

Verdict

The results behave like random draws. The best part of the method, the ranked FINAL 4, beats chance by a small margin: 41 of 718 live rounds = 5.7% against 4% (z = 2.3). No list, no agreement of lists, and no rule learned from the operations history gives more than that. A run of 7 hits in 7 rounds is out of reach for any list shorter than nearly all 100 numbers.

6. Audit of the scanner: can it find a pattern that is there?

Fake charts were built with a hidden rule planted in part of the moves ("after op A comes op B"). The same M1 scanner was run on them.

The scanner finds even a weak planted rule and more than doubles its hit rate. On the real charts it finds nothing, so the real operation history carries no repeating rule of that kind.

9. Conclusions for the project

  • Across 718 live rounds and 66,020 walk-forward steps, no method predicts the next number much better than chance.
  • The ranked FINAL 4 has a small edge (about ×1.4). It is real enough to keep but far from reliable.
  • Agreement between lists, wider lists, and rules learned from the operations history do not add accuracy.
  • The scanner's planted-pattern test shows the method would detect a rule if one existed, so the negative result is meaningful.
  • For the university submission, the defensible finding is this measured, walk-forward result with its honest range: FINAL 4 about 5–6% against 4% by chance.
