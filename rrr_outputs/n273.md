## 1b. Your point: 35 (c+2/−1) → +1/c−2 = 79

Yes: swap the sides of 35's op (−1/c+2) and flip both signs, and you get +1/c−2 = 79. That is the fourth swap/sign form from the N264 test (3.6% vs 3.4% chance).

This is now the third different way a FINAL 4 op has turned into the result after the fact (N264 swap + flip y, N267 the same, N272 swap + flip both). So I measured how much of the board this whole family of turns reaches each round, on 1078 replayed rounds:

| Turns allowed on the 4 FINAL 4 ops | Numbers reached each round | Result among them | Series N |
|---|---|---|---|
| swap sides and/or flip signs (8 forms) | **18 of 100** | **21%** | 20% |
| + cut moved or toggled (32 forms) | **51 of 100** | **54%** | 55% |
| + one step up or down | **98 of 100** | **98%** | 98% |

**What this means:** the hit rate equals the share of the board covered. Swap and sign flips alone reach about 18 numbers a round, so about 1 round in 5 the result is one of them, by plain chance. Add cut changes and half the board is reachable; add a step change and almost every number is. So after the result is known, there will nearly always be *some* turn of a FINAL 4 op that gives it. That is why each turn looks right on the round it is found and then fails the long test. The test is saved as `rrr_final4/transform_cover.py`. I keep tracking your rules 1–3, but I won't add new after-the-fact turns as rules, because their record can't be told apart from chance.
